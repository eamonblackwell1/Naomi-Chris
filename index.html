01
Friday 17 September

Arrival

Everyone flies into Kansai International rather than Tokyo. It is forty minutes from the airport into central Osaka, and it puts your guests an hour from the wedding venue instead of three. We handle transfers, hotel blocks and the group chat that inevitably forms the moment people start landing.

Nothing is scheduled for the first night. People arrive jet lagged at different hours and the kindest thing we can do is point them at a good bowl of noodles near the hotel and let them sleep.

02
Saturday 18 September

Osaka day and welcome dinner

First full day. Pick one of these for the group during the day. Welcome dinner in the evening.

Loud

Kishiwada Danjiri Matsuri

Massive wooden shrine floats hauled through the streets at a sprint, with riders dancing on the roof. Half a million people, lantern-lit floats at night. Happens this exact weekend, twenty five minutes from Namba.

Best for the photographers among us
Quiet

Nakazaki-cho and Shinsekai

Two old neighbourhoods that never got redeveloped. Nakazaki-cho is wooden houses and indie cafes five minutes from the skyscrapers. Shinsekai is kushikatsu alleys and retro signage. Both are walkable and worth photographing.

Walkable. Camera heavy. No crowds
Neon

Den-Den Town after dark

Osaka's anime and electronics quarter. Arcades, neon, Showa-era signage stacked six floors high. Best after dark when everything is lit.

Pairs with the welcome dinner nearby

The welcome dinner. Either a food village with a dozen stalls under one roof, or an evening in Tenma where locals actually drink and a hundred tiny bars sit shoulder to shoulder. We put money behind the bar.

03
Sunday 19 September

Day before the wedding

Something to see in the afternoon, an early night, then transfer to Kobe so everyone wakes up where they need to be.

Kyoto

Nishijin and the Shirakawa canal

Kyoto's old weaving district, preserved townhouses, then a willow-lined canal with stone bridges that empties out at sunset. Thirty minutes from Osaka.

Nara

Kasuga Taisha lantern corridor

Three thousand stone and bronze lanterns along a forest path to a shrine. Deer walk between them. There is a hall inside where the lanterns stay lit year round. Forty five minutes from Osaka.

Nothing

Rest day

Check into Kobe hotels, have a long lunch, go to bed early. Arrive at the wedding rested. Guests who want to explore get a list and go independently.

Can decide later
04
Monday 20 September

The wedding

Sorakuen is a walled Japanese garden in central Kobe with a pond, old pines, and a nineteenth century Western house. It is the courtyard setting you described, except licensed and staffed for weddings. Late afternoon ceremony in the garden, then everyone moves inside for the reception.

Black dress for the ceremony, pastel pink for the reception. The whole day is built around that switch.

Ceremony

Garden, at the water

Chairs on the lawn facing the pond, Western officiant, open sky. Ceremony at four or five in the afternoon when the heat drops.

Reception

Long table, lit from above

One table, everyone at it, lanterns and candles. Works up to about thirty five guests. Beyond that it becomes round tables.

Reception

Dinner, then party

Seated dinner and speeches, then tables move and the lights drop for a DJ. We confirm the venue's sound curfew in writing before you sign.

Alternative venue

Sola Kobe

Sola is in Kobe's Kitano district, run by the same company as Sorakuen. Our pricing and relationship carry across.

Where Sorakuen looks inward at a garden, Sola looks outward: a stone and glass chapel open to the sky, a rooftop lawn ceremony over the city, and three private villas for the reception. Western ceremony by design.

Grace Villa was renovated in 2024. For thirty guests it would be exclusively yours. For fifty, Monsoon Villa works. Both have indoor and outdoor space, so weather is not a problem. Five minutes by car from Sorakuen.

The difference. Sorakuen is a garden. Sola is architecture and a city view. Both are Western format, both in Kobe, same operator.

The Look

Look and feel

Lanterns, pink, black, and how we handle the cherry blossom question in September.

Flowers

Cosmos, not sakura

Cosmos blooms across Kansai in September and October, comes in the pale pink you asked for, and its Japanese name translates as autumn cherry blossom. In season, local, affordable.

Flowers

Installed blossom

If you want literal cherry blossom, Japanese venues build it out of season routinely. A blossom arch or branches above the table. Budget decision, not an availability one.

Light

Warm and low

Paper lanterns over the table, hanging lanterns in the trees, candles at eye level. Nothing white, nothing overhead, nothing that flattens a face.

Dresses

Black into pink

Black in daylight against green reads sharp. Pink after dark under warm light reads soft. We build the florals and lighting around that transition.

05
Tuesday 21 September

Recovery day in Kobe

Late brunch, then the day splits. Some people want a walk, some want to drive. Kobe beef dinner in the evening.

Slow

Kitano ijinkan

Hillside of preserved Meiji-era Western merchant houses. Tudor, French and German architecture above a Japanese city with the sea below. Fifteen minutes on foot from Sannomiya.

Fast

Mount Rokko, then the night view

Real mountain passes thirty minutes from the venue. Tight switchbacks, forest, summit. Ends at the Mount Maya lookout, one of the three great night views in Japan.

International Driving Permits needed before you fly
Still

Arima Onsen

Hot spring town on the far side of Mount Rokko, thirty minutes from Kobe. Gold and silver mineral baths, old streetscape. Private family baths available.

Dinner. Kobe beef, at a family run teppanyaki counter with the chef cooking in front of you rather than a large restaurant with a famous name. We book a private room or take the whole counter so it stays a group dinner and not a queue.

Friday 24 September

The car day

Friday deliberately. The meets peak on Friday and Saturday nights, and this keeps Saturday free for the farewell.

Morning

Kanpachi-dori dealer row

Specialist dealers through west Tokyo. Stock in the windows includes LFAs, 2000GTs, Countachs and delivery-mileage R32s. No ticket, no museum. Walk in and look.

Afternoon

Yokohama

Nissan HQ gallery on the waterfront for heritage GT-Rs, then Honmoku for the hot rod scene near the old US base. Twenty five minutes from central Tokyo.

Golden hour

Kawasaki industrial zone

Petrochemical complex on artificial islands that looks like Blade Runner after dark. Reached by boat or chartered coach. Good for photographers.

Night

Daikoku PA

The expressway parking area where the scene gathers. Skylines next to Aventadors under floodlights, hoods up. No pedestrian access, so we charter vehicles and go in as a group.

Alternative

Drive it yourself

Rent an R34 GT-R, FD RX-7 or Mk4 Supra and drive the Hakone Turnpike. This becomes its own full day, swapping in for the free day.

Permits required

Higashi Osaka parking area on Saturday night, plus the elevated highway loop the street racers made famous, now driven legally behind a lead car.

Farewell and honeymoon

Last brunch, airport transfers, then the week that is just yours. Three directions.

Mountains

Hakone and Fuji

Ninety minutes from Tokyo. Ryokan with a private open-air bath, dinner in your room, Fuji across the lake on a clear morning.

Islands

Seto Inland Sea

Naoshima and Teshima: contemporary art and architecture built into the landscape, almost empty midweek in September. Beaches, ferries, quiet.

Beach

Okinawa

Subtropical, three hours by air. Late September is the tail of typhoon season down there, so we would build in flexible dates.
